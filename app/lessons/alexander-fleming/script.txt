00:07 Today, I would like to tell you about a very special person in science – Sir Alexander Fleming. So, who was Alexander Fleming?
00:16 He was a Scottish doctor and scientist. He was born on August 6th, 1881, in Scotland.
00:24 He loved science and medicine. He wanted to help people and make their lives better.
00:30 In 1928, Fleming was working in his laboratory. One day, he noticed something unusual. A kind of mould had grown on one of his bacteria plates. Around the mould, the bacteria had died.
00:44 Fleming did not ignore this strange thing. He studied it carefully. He discovered a substance that could stop bacteria from growing. He called it penicillin.
00:55 Penicillin later became a very important medicine. It has saved millions of lives.
01:01 In 1945, Fleming, together with Ernst Chain and Howard Florey, received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the discovery of penicillin and its effects against infectious diseases.
01:14 But why do I admire Alexander Fleming?
01:17 First, I admire his curiosity. He always asked questions and wanted to understand how things worked. When he saw something unusual, he did not simply walk away. He looked carefully and asked, “Why?”
01:31 Second, I admire his wisdom and love of learning. He studied science for many years. He used his knowledge to help people and improve medicine.
01:41 Third, I admire his determination. His discovery started with something unexpected. But he kept studying, testing, and learning. He showed us that a small observation can lead to a big idea.
01:55 For me, Alexander Fleming is not only a great scientist. He is also a great example for young people.
02:02 From him, I learn three important things: Be curious. Keep learning. And never give up.
02:09 I may be only a third grader today, but I can start with small things: asking questions, studying hard, and helping others.
02:17 That is why Alexander Fleming is the person who made history that I admire. Thank you for listening!
